# Dial counter generator

Print a dial counter, or life counter, that tracks life totals, wounds, victory points, or anything else. By default it has two ten-sided dials, tens on the left and units on the right, counting 00 to 99, that click from count to count with no magnets or other hardware. The count is the top row, between the marks on the two end plates.

It is an axle, two end plates and the dials. Each dial clicks on a spring arm inside it, whose round tip drops between the ridges of the axle. To put it together, press the axle into one plate, slide the dials on with the rib on each outer dial's wider end facing out, and press the other plate on. The plates' sockets are the axle's own shape, so it presses in tight and cannot turn.

Choose one to four dials and how many numbers each carries, from 6 to 20, starting from any number: one dial from 1 to 16, say, or three dials counting 000 to 999. More numbers make a bigger dial. Count down puts the numbers the other way round, so rolling a dial forwards takes one off.

Size scales the whole counter, measured across a dial's flats; the default is 34.95mm, which makes the counter about 28mm wide and 37mm tall. The fits stay the same at every size.

The digits are inlaid by default: engraved into the dials and filled underneath with the second filament, which also prints each plate's mark over the top row. Engraved for paint leaves the digits empty and prints everything in one filament. Raised stands the digits on the faces in the second filament. With advanced settings shown you can set how deep the digits go, or how tall raised ones stand, and how deep the inlay is. The top-row mark can be a bar, a pointer or nothing. Each plate's outer face can carry text and an uploaded image, inlaid flush in the second colour; it prints on the build plate, so the colour changes only for its first layers. The plates can print in a third filament of their own. In the 3MF, set up for a two-nozzle Bambu Lab X2D, every part sits on one plate with its colour already assigned.

The fit settings are advanced too. Axle press fit opens the plates' sockets, which are otherwise exactly the axle's size: raise it if the axle will not go in. Dial play is the gap between each dial and the axle. Click moves each spring arm's tip further into the axle's ridges: raise it if the dials turn too easily, and lower it if they are hard to turn. Lead-in chamfers the ends of the axle and the mouths of the sockets so the press fit starts more easily. The 3MF tells Bambu Studio to keep the axle's first layer from spreading, since that end presses into a plate.

Every part prints without supports: the axle on its end, the plates flat, and each dial on its narrow end. Pick the colours from the swatches at the top of the page: they apply to every generator, are saved in your browser, and go into the 3MF.
