# Dial counter generator

Print a dial counter, or wound caddy, that tracks wounds, victory points, command points, or anything else that counts from 00 to 99. Two ten-sided dials, tens on the left and units on the right, click from count to count with no magnets or other hardware. The count is the top row.

Layout picks how the dials are held and turned:

- **Enclosure**, the default, puts the dials in a ten-sided case with a window over the top row, so only the count shows. You turn each dial by a ribbed knob outside its end. The case prints as two halves that slot together in the middle. It is six parts plus the tiles: two case halves, two dials and two knobs, one of which carries a rod that runs through both dials.
- **Hubs and knobs** is the same, with open hubs the size of the dials instead of a case, and a black bracket inlaid in each hub that frames the top row.
- **Hubs on legs** turns the dials directly. They click on a tube that is part of the left hub; the right hub presses on to its free end. A bracket in each hub frames the top row.
- **Frame and base** turns the dials directly too. A black frame boxes the top row and stands on a post past each dial, which holds the tube and plugs into a base plate under the dials.

Every count clicks into place on two spring arms that grip a ridged shaft or tube from both sides. The click setting is how deep the ridges are: raise it if the dials turn too easily, and lower it if they are hard to turn. Grip makes each dial longer, and its tiles with it. Pick a size to scale the whole counter, digits included; Wheel diameter sets an exact size. Full size is the default; the enclosure is then about 48mm wide and 44mm tall, with digits about 7.6mm tall. The fit clearance is the play round each moving part.

There are three ways to do the digits. Press-in tiles, the default, print the digits on a plate of their own. Each face gets a tile the size of its pocket, running the dial's whole length, with the digit and a line above and below it raised in the second filament. In the 3MF, set up for a two-nozzle Bambu Lab X2D, every tile is its own object, named by its wheel and digit, so to reprint a few that failed you can delete the rest from the plate. The brackets print on the same plate, black above a white foot as tall as a tile's base, so the plate changes filament once and every other part prints in one colour. Press each tile into its pocket and it sits flush with the face, with no glue; raise the insert clearance if tiles will not go in. Printed in the dial makes each digit a coloured part of the dial itself, with no tiles, but the printer changes filament on every layer of the dials. Engraved for paint cuts shallow digits you fill by hand. Digit filament sets whether the digits share the second filament with the brackets and frame, or take a third of their own.

Every part prints without supports. Pick the colours from the swatches at the top of the page: they apply to every generator, are saved in your browser, and go into the 3MF. Parts that would have little on the plate get a 6mm brim in the 3MF automatically.
