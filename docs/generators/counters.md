# Dial counter generator

Print a dial counter that tracks wounds, victory points, command points, or anything else that counts from 00 to 99. Two ten-sided drums, tens on the left and units on the right, each rest on one flat with the count on the flat on top. Roll a drum onto its next flat to count up or down. A thin pointer between the drums stands on a wide flat base, so it stays upright while they roll, and the arrows on its top mark the count.

Every count clicks into place with no magnets or other hardware. Each drum has ten small cone bumps on its inner face, and the pointer has ten matching dimples in each face. At each count the bumps sit in the dimples. To turn a drum, the bumps must ride up out of the dimples, which pushes the drum outward and squeezes the hub's split barb, so the drum stays put when you pick the counter up or put it down. The click setting is how far the barb gives as each count passes: raise it if the drums turn too easily, and lower it if they are hard to turn.

Turn on colour inserts to print the digits and arrows in a second filament. Each face gets a tile the size of its pocket with the digit raised on it, so it has plenty of area to hold the build plate. The tiles print face up on a plate of their own. In the 3MF, set up for a single-nozzle Bambu Lab P2S, each tile's base is on filament 1 and its raised digit on filament 2, in the colour you pick; no layer holds both, so the printer changes filament once. The STL download has the tile bases and the raised digits as two files; load them together as one object with two parts and give the digits the second filament. Press each tile into its pocket and the digit sits flush with the face. Each tile is smaller than its pocket by the insert clearance all round: raise it if tiles will not go in, and lower it or add a drop of glue if they fall out. Turn inserts off to engrave the digits and arrows for paint instead.

Set the drum diameter and width, and the fit clearance, which is the play around the hub and the gap either side of the pointer. The bumps grow with the clearance, so the click stays the same. Every part prints without supports, each lying on its outer face, with the units drum's hub standing up. To assemble, press in the tiles, then push the units drum's hub through the pointer and then the tens drum until its split barb snaps into the tens drum's outer face.
